### Situation

Mamaearth’s regional operations and finance review established a verified cleaned total
revenue of ₹97,358.30, revised downward from the raw total revenue of ₹99,860.20 following
a duplicate reconciliation difference of ₹2,501.90. Performance analysis across channels
indicates varying return rates by payment method: credit and debit card orders recorded a
return rate of 14.7%, while UPI orders experienced a return rate of 18.9%. Additionally,
adjusting the monthly performance data resolved earlier reporting distortions. In January
2026, an outlier produced an apparent revenue of ₹29,582.10, whereas the corrected revenue
stood at ₹11,637.10. Consequently, the true peak month for the period was March 2026,
which generated revenue of ₹20,318.90.

### Complication

The primary operational risk stems from cash-on-delivery (COD) orders, where the overall
return rate reached 44.4%. Risk concentration is most acute in the highest-risk
segment—Tier-2 cities using COD—where the return rate reached 54.5%. Because the current
findings measure return percentages rather than specific downstream costs, it is not
established whether these returns have directly reduced profit margins, stranded inventory
in transit, or created specific financial losses. Instead, these elevated return rates
represent significant operational risks and analytical blind spots that require thorough
investigation to understand their practical effect on unit economics and logistics
fulfillment.

### Resolution

Finance and regional operations teams should immediately implement the verified baseline
of ₹97,358.30 for financial planning and utilize March 2026 (₹20,318.90) as the accurate
performance benchmark. Operationally, leadership should initiate an inquiry into COD
fulfillment, specifically focusing on the Tier-2 COD customer segment with its 54.5%
return rate. Practical next steps include auditing the delivery partner workflows,
analyzing order verification protocols before dispatch, and investigating the operational
and financial impact of returns on working capital and inventory health. These measures
will clarify the exact cost mechanisms behind COD returns without relying on unverified
assumptions about margin erosion or transit holding times.
